For something like \(m=1\) and \(n=1\) we have the line \(y = g(x) = x\text{.}\) The graph is a straight line on standard Cartesian coordinates, tilted upward from left to right at a \(45^{\circ}\) angle. For powers like \(n=2\) or \(n=3\text{,}\) we have power functions representing the leading terms of quadratic or cubic functions. For somewhat unusal powers of \(0 \le n \le 1\text{,}\) like \(n = \frac{1}{2}\) or \(n = \frac{1}{3}\) we have power functions like the square root or cube root, respectively.
