Example 2.3.2. The derivative of a quartic.

Using the limit definition and making use of Pascal’s Triangle to find coefficients in the necessary expansion of \((x+h)^{4}\text{,}\) we have,
\begin{align*} f'(x) & = \lim\limits_{h\to0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}\frac{(x+h)^4 - (x^4)}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}\frac{x^4+4x^{3}h + 6x^{2}h^2 + 4xh^{3} + h^{4}- x^4}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}\frac{4x^{3}h + 6x^{2}h^{2} + 4xh^{3}+ h^4}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}\frac{h(4x^{3} + 6x^{2}h + 4xh^{2} + h^{3})}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}4x^{3} + 6x^{2}h + 4xh^{2} + h^{3}\\ f'(x) & = 4x^{3} + 6x^{2}(0) + 4x(0)^{2} + (0)^{3} = 4x^{3} \end{align*}
