Theorem 2.3.4. Power rule for derivatives.

For a power function \(f(x) = x^{n}\) and any nonzero power \(n\text{,}\) the derivative is given by
\begin{equation*} f'(x) = \dfrac{d}{dx}\Big(x^{n}\Big) = nx^{n-1}\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.

The proof is contained in the limit definition of the derivative. Recall that when we expanded \((x+h)^2\) we found \((x+h)^2 = 1x^2 + 2xh + 1h^2\text{,}\) and for \((x+h)^3 = 1x^3 + 3x^{2}h + 3xh^{2} + 1h^3\text{,}\) and finally, \((x+h)^4 = 1x^4 + 4x^{3}h + 6x^{2}h^{2} + 4xh^{3} + 1h^4\text{.}\) The first terms combine with the corresponding power of \(x\) that is subtracted in the numerator, and the term that remains has a coefficient that matches the order of the original power in the binomial expansion. Writing out the first few rows of Pascal’s Triangle might give you some more confidence in this pattern.
🔗
\begin{align*} f'(x) & = \lim\limits_{h\to0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}\frac{(x+h)^n - (x)^n}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}\frac{x^n+nx^{n-1}h + \cdots + nxh^{n-1} + h^{n}- x^n}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}\frac{nx^{n-1}h + \cdots + nxh^{n-1} + h^{n}}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}\frac{h(nx^{n-1} + \cdots + nxh^{n-2} + h^{n-1})}{h}\\ & = \lim\limits_{h\to0}nx^{n-1} + \cdots + nxh^{n-2} + h^{n-1}\\ f'(x) & = nx^{n-1} + \cdots + nx(0)^{n-2} + (0)^{n-1} = nx^{n-1} \end{align*}
In taking the limit as \(h \to 0\) all of the terms that still contain \(h\) vanish and all that remains is the term that only involves \(x\) but not \(h\text{.}\)
